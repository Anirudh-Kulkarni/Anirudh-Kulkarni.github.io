Intro

👋 Hi there, I’m Anirudh (Ani). I work at the intersection of technology, product experience, and business strategy. At MathWorks, I partner with engineering, UX, and product marketing teams to shape the user experience, customer workflows, and technical documentation for agentic AI and cloud products (the MATLAB Agentic Toolkit, AWS, Azure, and Docker). I hold a PhD in computational neuroscience and am pursuing an MBA at Imperial College Business School. Explore my portfolio to see selected work.

At MathWorks (as a Senior Technical Writer), I work across product UX and technical communication to help engineers and scientists adopt agentic AI workflows, cloud computing (AWS, Azure, Docker, and Kubernetes), and deep learning in the cloud.

Alongside creating workflow guides and an end-to-end video walkthrough, I contribute UX designs, customer-journey maps, user research feedback, and interface copy (tooltips, warnings, and cost/deletion confirmations) to MathWorks Cloud Center, and use usage analytics to prioritise product and content improvements. Check out my Portfolio for selected work.

I am pursuing a part-time MBA at Imperial College Business School (2026–2027), focusing on Strategy, Decision Analytics, Innovation & Entrepreneurship, Finance & Accounting, Managerial Economics, Marketing, Macroeconomics, and Organisational Behaviour.

Before joining MathWorks, I worked as a researcher in computational and experimental neuroscience, using mathematical models and custom algorithms in Python and MATLAB to make sense of complex, high-dimensional brain data. Check out my research page for accessible summaries of my six peer-reviewed publications:

  • Imperial College London (Department of Bioengineering): As a Research Associate with Dr. Tobias Reichenbach, I modelled how the primary auditory cortex combines what we hear with what we see (like moving lips) to help us understand speech in noisy places (first author, Journal of Neural Engineering, 2021).
  • INSERM, Institut de la Vision (Paris): As a Postdoctoral Researcher with Dr. Romain Brette, I studied Paramecium (a single-celled organism that swims around and fires electrical spikes much like a brain cell) by building a microfluidic device to hold the cell still (first author, Journal of Experimental Biology, 2020) and modelling how its electrical activity drove its movement.
